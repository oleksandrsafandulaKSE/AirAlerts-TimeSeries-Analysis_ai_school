    All 24 oblasts now, and how the live forecast works
    • Every minute a scheduled job asks alerts.in.ua which alerts are active, and fetches one oblast's alert history for the past month (24 oblasts in rotation, so each is re-checked about every 24 minutes). The forecast covers the 60 minutes after that moment and uses only what was known then.
    • Model: the study's "offset" model (two-week rate plus learned corrections), trained only on official data up to July 2026. No retraining, no tuning on the new data.
    • "Alert running": an alert started less than 24 hours ago is still active, so there is no forecast of a new alert. The end of an alert is confirmed by the history check, so this can lag the real end by up to about 24 minutes. A red outline means alerts.in.ua lists an alert as active right now (alerts older than 24 hours count as background and the oblast is still forecast).
    • An alert that starts and ends between two checks is only seen after that oblast's next history check (at most about 24 minutes).
    • "Data gap": if the feed was missed for more than 5 minutes, inputs that look back over the gap are unknown and no forecast is shown until every oblast's history check has covered the gap (about 24 minutes), or, if the history cannot be fetched, until the gap is more than 8 days old. A record the feed sent in a broken form also marks its oblast as a data gap for 24 hours. Inputs are never filled in by guessing.
    • "Caution": the two-week rate rests on fewer than 48 forecastable hours, or it is higher than almost anything the model saw in training (above 65%; in training 99.9% of values were lower). Counting all alert types, frontline oblasts are under alert nearly all the time, so their two-week rate can reach 100% from a few hours.
    • If the last update failed or is more than 5 minutes old, the map turns grey.
    • Luhanska oblast (a permanent alert since 2022) and Crimea are not forecast.
    The study · official data, 2022 – September 2026 · 23 oblasts + Kyiv City

    Will a new air-raid alert start in the next hour?

    A model gives every oblast, every hour, a chance that a new alert starts in the next hour. It only uses what was known at that moment. We tested it once, on six weeks of real alerts it had never seen, against a simple rule: "use how often alerts started in the last two weeks".

    Short answer

      "Accuracy" here is the Brier score, which rewards honest chances. A forecast counts only for hours with no recent alert running; an alert counts as running only in its first 24 hours.

      Limitations

      What these results cannot tell you

      • The test period is short (six weeks) and loses several days to the recording gap; frontline groups are low-n.
      • The rolling recent-rate baseline adapts during the test while the frozen model does not.
      • No text, Telegram or weather inputs; one pooled model per horizon; no hyperparameter search.
      • Recording outages that hit only some oblasts cannot be detected from the data.
      • At three hours, neighbouring hours share label windows, so they are not independent observations.
      • The new-regime check uses a different source, one month of data, and an open question about which alert types count.
      • The pre-registration and the access marker are local files; they become tamper-evident only once committed.

      Data: Vadimkin/ukrainian-air-raid-sirens-dataset (official feed, deduplicated) and alerts.in.ua (exploratory). Every number on this page is read from files written by the project's pipeline; nothing is typed in by hand.
